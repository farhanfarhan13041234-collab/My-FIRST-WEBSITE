What Is an MRI Machine?

MRI stands for Magnetic Resonance Imaging. It is a medical imaging technology that uses a strong magnetic field, radio waves and computer processing to create detailed pictures of the inside of the human body.

Doctors use MRI scans to examine the brain, spine, muscles, joints, organs and other body structures.

Interesting fact: MRI does not use ionising radiation like X-rays and CT scans.

Main Parts of an MRI Machine

🧲

1. Main Magnet

Produces the powerful, stable magnetic field needed for MRI imaging. Many modern scanners use superconducting magnets.

〰️

2. Gradient Coils

Produce controlled changes in the magnetic field. These help the scanner identify where signals come from inside the body.

📡

3. RF Coils

Radiofrequency coils transmit and receive radio signals used to create MRI images.

🛏️

4. Patient Table

Supports the patient and moves them into the scanner under the control of trained staff.

💻

5. Computer System

Processes the signals received by the scanner and reconstructs medical images.

❄️

6. Cooling System

Some superconducting MRI magnets use cryogenic cooling, commonly involving liquid helium.

🏗️

7. Magnet Housing

The outer structure supports and protects the magnet and related equipment.

🔩

8. Nuts and Bolts

Specialised fasteners are used in appropriate structural assemblies. Their materials and specifications must be engineered for MRI compatibility and safety.

🛡️

9. Magnetic Shielding

The scanner room is designed to control electromagnetic interference and manage the magnetic field environment.

How Does an MRI Machine Work?

Step 1: Magnetic Field

The main magnet creates a strong magnetic field that influences hydrogen nuclei in the body.

Step 2: Radiofrequency Signals

Radiofrequency pulses interact with the hydrogen nuclei and change their magnetic orientation.

Step 3: Signal Reception

As the nuclei return towards equilibrium, they produce signals detected by RF coils.

Step 4: Image Processing

The computer processes the collected signals using mathematical techniques.

Step 5: Medical Images

The computer reconstructs images that qualified medical professionals can examine.

How Are MRI Machines Manufactured?

MRI machines are complex medical devices made by specialised engineering and medical technology companies. Their manufacture requires several engineering disciplines.

1. Engineering Design

Engineers design the magnet, electronics, mechanical structure and imaging systems.

2. Magnet Production

Specialised manufacturers produce and test the magnet system and its components.

3. Mechanical Components

The frame, housing, patient table and other structures are manufactured using controlled industrial processes.

4. Electronic Systems

Electronic components, gradient systems, RF systems and computer controls are developed.

5. Assembly

Qualified technicians assemble the machine following the manufacturer's approved procedures.

6. Testing

Engineers conduct electrical, magnetic, mechanical and image-quality testing before clinical installation.

Engineering safety: MRI magnets, cryogenic systems and high-power electronics require specialised facilities and trained professionals. This overview is educational, not a DIY manufacturing guide.

How Is MRI Used in Hospitals?

1. Medical Assessment

A healthcare professional determines whether an MRI examination is appropriate.

2. Safety Screening

Staff ask about implants, medical devices, metal objects and other relevant safety concerns.

3. Patient Preparation

The patient follows staff instructions and removes objects that are not permitted in the MRI environment.

4. MRI Examination

Trained staff operate the scanner and monitor the patient during the examination.

5. Image Review

A qualified radiologist interprets the images and prepares a medical report.

MRI Safety

MRI safety is extremely important because the magnetic field can attract certain metal objects with great force.

Strong Magnetic Field

Some metal objects can become dangerous projectiles near an MRI scanner.

Medical Implants

Patients must tell staff about implants, pacemakers and medical devices. Compatibility must be checked.

Hearing Protection

MRI scanners can be loud. Appropriate hearing protection is provided to patients.

Trained Professionals

Only authorised and trained professionals should operate or service MRI equipment.

Never enter an MRI scanner room with an object unless authorised MRI staff have confirmed it is safe.

Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Detailed soft-tissue images
  • No ionising radiation
  • Useful for brain and spine imaging
  • Can examine joints and organs

Disadvantages

  • Can be expensive
  • Some examinations take time
  • Can be noisy
  • Not suitable for every patient
  • Requires specialist staff

Interesting MRI Facts

Magnet Strength

Many hospital MRI scanners operate at 1.5 Tesla or 3 Tesla.

Hydrogen Atoms

MRI commonly detects signals from hydrogen nuclei found throughout the human body.

Different Images

Different MRI sequences can highlight different tissue characteristics.
